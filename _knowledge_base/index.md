## <span id="medical-condition-guides">📖 Medical Condition Guides</span>

### [Understanding Low Back Pain Claims at WSIB](low-back-pain-claims.html)

**Based on 194 cases (62% of all conditions)**

Low back pain is the #1 workplace injury in WSIB appeals. This guide covers:
- How to prove work-relatedness (the hardest part)
- Dealing with pre-existing conditions
- What medical evidence tribunals accept
- Timeline for appeal process

**Read this if:** You have a back injury and WSIB denied your claim or benefits.

---

### [Chronic Pain: Building Your Case](chronic-pain-claims.html)

**Based on 186 cases (2nd most common)**

Chronic pain is real, but WSIB often denies it as "subjective" or "disproportionate to injury." This guide shows you:
- How to document functional limitations
- Medical explanations that work
- Defeating the "pain is subjective" denial
- Treatment compliance requirements

**Read this if:** You have ongoing pain that WSIB says is "all in your head."

---

### [Pre-Existing Conditions: What You Need to Know](pre-existing-conditions.html)

**Based on 96 cases**

WSIB's #1 denial reason: "This is pre-existing, not work-related." This guide explains:
- The "thin skull rule" (you don't need a perfect body)
- Aggravation vs. acceleration (legal terms that matter)
- How to prove work made it worse
- Apportionment (when WSIB tries to split the blame)

**Read this if:** WSIB denied you because you had an old injury or condition.

---

### [Psychotraumatic Disability: Understanding Your Rights](psychotraumatic-disability.html)

**Based on 92 psychotraumatic + 74 PTSD cases**

Mental injuries are covered by WSIB—but only if you meet specific criteria. This guide covers:
- Traumatic event vs. workplace stress (only one is covered)
- How to prove causation
- Medical evidence for PTSD, anxiety, depression
- Timeline and treatment requirements

**Read this if:** You have PTSD, anxiety, or depression from a workplace event.

---

### [Understanding Permanent Impairment Ratings](permanent-impairment-rating.html)

**Based on 74 cases**

Permanent impairment gets you a lump sum (NEL award), but the rating system is confusing. This guide explains:
- How ratings work (AMA Guides)
- Medical impairment vs. economic disability
- How to appeal a low rating
- What financial benefits you're entitled to

**Read this if:** WSIB gave you a permanent impairment rating and you think it's too low.

---

### [Fibromyalgia and WSIB: Your Complete Guide](fibromyalgia-claims.html)

**Based on 68 cases (one of the hardest conditions to win)**

Fibromyalgia is real and can be work-related—but proving it is difficult. This guide shows you:
- Diagnostic criteria tribunals accept
- Post-traumatic fibromyalgia (work-triggered)
- Medical evidence strategy
- How to defeat the "pre-existing" argument

**Read this if:** You have fibromyalgia and WSIB denied your claim.

## <span id="how-to-use">How to Use These Guides</span>

### Step 1: Find Your Condition

Pick the guide that matches your situation:
- **Back injury?** → Low Back Pain guide
- **Ongoing pain?** → Chronic Pain guide
- **Old injury made worse?** → Pre-Existing Conditions guide
- **PTSD/mental injury?** → Psychotraumatic Disability guide
- **Low impairment rating?** → Permanent Impairment Rating guide
- **Fibromyalgia?** → Fibromyalgia guide

### Step 2: Read the Whole Article

Don't skip sections. The evidence requirements build on each other.

### Step 3: Download the Matching Template

If WSIB denied your claim, use the template to write your appeal:
- [Back Injury Template](../templates/back-injury-appeal.html)
- [Chronic Pain Template](../templates/chronic-pain-appeal.html)
- [Pre-Existing Condition Template](../templates/pre-existing-appeal.html)

### Step 4: Get Local Help

**Free legal resources:**
- [Community Legal Assistance Ontario](https://www.legalaid.on.ca/) - Free legal help if you qualify
- IAVGO (Injured and Disabled Workers) - Peer support and advocacy
- Local legal clinics (see Thunder Bay section below)

**You don't need to do this alone.** These guides give you knowledge, but local advocates can help you apply it.

## <span id="data-transparency">📊 Data Transparency</span>

### How We Built This

**Data collection:** April 7-8, 2026
- Source: [CanLII Ontario WSIAT decisions](https://www.canlii.org/en/on/onwsiat/)
- Method: Sequential case ID enumeration (API search doesn't exist—learned the hard way!)
- Cases collected: 1,204 (118 from 2026, 1,086 from 2025)
- Total cases screened: 19,032
- Relevant disability cases: 1,334 (7% match rate)

**Pattern analysis:**
- Top 20 keywords extracted
- Condition breakdown calculated
- Success factors identified (limited by metadata quality)

**Limitations:**
- ✅ **Ontario only** (for now—BC, Quebec, Alberta coming soon)
- ✅ **Metadata-based** (don't have full case text yet—96% unknown outcomes)
- ✅ **Pattern analysis** (shows what evidence appears, not guaranteed outcomes)
- ✅ **Educational only** (not legal advice—always consult a professional)

**Read the full story:** [Building a Knowledge Base From Tribunal Decisions](../blog/2026-04-08-building-knowledge-base-from-tribunal-decisions.html) - Transparency blog post showing the API failures, pivots, and how we actually built this.
