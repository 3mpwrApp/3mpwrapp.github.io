Ontario Court of Appeal: 5,034 appellate decisions analyzed — injured workers and disability community members appealing from lower courts/tribunals face outcome obscurity at highest provincial court level.
💡 Who ONCA Serves: ONCA is Ontario's highest court for provincial matters. Hears appeals from:
  • WSIAT appeals: Injured workers challenging WSIAT decisions on questions of law (judicial review)
  • ONSBT judicial reviews: People with disabilities challenging ONSBT ODSP/OW decisions
  • HRTO judicial reviews: Discrimination claimants challenging HRTO decisions
  • Lower court appeals: Civil, criminal, family law matters

Why Outcome Clarity Matters at Appellate Level: ONCA decisions set binding legal precedent for all Ontario tribunals and lower courts. When outcomes are unclear, it's impossible to:

  • Understand which tribunal decisions are upheld vs overturned
  • Identify emerging legal principles affecting injured workers and disability community
  • Track whether appellate courts are expanding or restricting worker/disability rights
  • Assess whether judicial review is worth pursuing (time, cost, complexity)

Cross-Community Impact: ONCA decisions affect BOTH injured workers (via WSIAT/WSIB appeals) AND people with disabilities (via ONSBT/ODSP and HRTO appeals).

📊 About ONCA (Ontario Court of Appeal)

What ONCA Does: Highest court in Ontario for provincial law. Reviews lower court decisions and tribunal decisions on appeals and judicial reviews. Decisions are binding precedent for all Ontario courts and tribunals.

Common Disability/Worker Justice Cases:

  • WSIAT Judicial Reviews: Questions of law from WSIAT decisions (entitlement, causation, permanent impairment)
  • ONSBT Judicial Reviews: ODSP/OW eligibility, benefit calculation, "substantial restriction" standard
  • HRTO Judicial Reviews: Discrimination findings, accommodation duty, remedies
  • Civil Appeals: Personal injury, wrongful dismissal, insurance disputes

Why ONCA Outcomes Matter for Disability Justice:

  • ONCA interpretations of "disability," "entitlement," "accommodation duty" affect thousands of cases
  • Appellate decisions clarify (or obscure) rights under Workplace Safety and Insurance Act, Ontario Disability Support Program Act, Ontario Human Rights Code
  • Outcome obscurity at appellate level means precedent-setting cases are invisible to community

📊 Data & Methodology

Data Source: 5,034 ONCA decisions from CanLII (2020-2026), analyzed May 2026

Year-by-Year Breakdown:

  • 2020: 840 decisions
  • 2021: 924 decisions
  • 2022: 890 decisions
  • 2023: 849 decisions
  • 2024: 314 decisions
  • 2025: 896 decisions
  • 2026 (partial): 321 decisions (through May)

Note on 2024 Data: Lower volume may reflect data processing gaps or tribunal backlog affecting appellate caseload.

Outcome Classification Method: Keyword-based inference from decision text. "Clear outcome" = decision contains outcome indicators ("appeal allowed," "dismissed," "granted," "denied," "leave to appeal granted/refused"). "Unclear outcome" = no standard outcome language found.

Limitation: CanLII API responses do not include standardized structured outcome labels. Keyword-based classification is approximate. ~75-85% of ONCA decisions have unclear outcomes. Many ONCA decisions are procedural (leave to appeal motions, stays, interim orders) which may lack clear outcome language.

ONCA Decision Types:

  • Leave to Appeal Decisions: Most common. ONCA decides whether to hear appeal. "Leave granted" = appeal proceeds. "Leave refused" = appeal ends.
  • Appeal Decisions (Merits): Full appeal hearing. "Appeal allowed" = lower decision overturned. "Appeal dismissed" = lower decision upheld.
  • Procedural Orders: Stays, adjournments, case management orders
