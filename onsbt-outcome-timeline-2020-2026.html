📊 About ONSBT (Ontario Social Benefits Tribunal)

What ONSBT Does: Independent administrative tribunal hearing appeals from:

  • ODSP (Ontario Disability Support Program): Income support for people with disabilities. Appeals involve eligibility denials, benefit amount disputes, program participation requirements.
  • Ontario Works (OW): Social assistance program. Appeals involve eligibility, assistance amounts, participation requirements.

Who Uses ONSBT: People with disabilities (physical, mental, developmental) denied ODSP; people experiencing poverty denied OW; family members/advocates supporting appellants.

Common Appeal Reasons:

  • ODSP eligibility denied (medical condition not "severe and prolonged")
  • ODSP benefits cut or suspended
  • Ontario Works participation requirements too restrictive
  • Overpayment disputes

📊 Data & Methodology

Data Source: 13,798 ONSBT decisions from CanLII (2020-2026), analyzed May 2026

Year-by-Year Breakdown:

  • 2020: 1,687 decisions
  • 2021: 2,186 decisions
  • 2022: 2,456 decisions
  • 2023: 2,687 decisions
  • 2024: 2,398 decisions
  • 2025: 1,876 decisions
  • 2026 (partial): 508 decisions (through May)

Outcome Classification Method: Keyword-based inference from decision text. "Clear outcome" = decision contains outcome indicators ("allowed," "dismissed," "granted," "denied," "withdrawn"). "Unclear outcome" = no standard outcome language found.

Limitation: CanLII API responses do not include standardized structured outcome labels. Keyword-based classification is approximate. ~75-85% of ONSBT decisions have unclear outcomes. To get 100% accurate outcomes, would require manual reading of each decision.

Why This Matters for Disability Justice:

  • People with disabilities navigating ODSP appeals face information barriers
  • Outcome obscurity prevents pattern identification (which medical evidence types work, which denial reasons can be overturned)
  • Transparency gaps disproportionately harm people with cognitive disabilities, limited literacy, no legal representation
  • This is a systemic accessibility failure in Ontario's disability benefits system
