**April 30, 2026** | Investigation by 3mpwrApp Data Team

## How We Discovered This

Last week, we downloaded every WSIAT decision ever published: [98,992 decisions from 1987-2026](https://www.wsiat.ca/en/home/opendata_decisions.html).

We found that **69% of WSIAT appeals succeed**. That's a remarkably high success rate—it means WSIB gets it wrong most of the time.

But something didn't add up. If WSIB denies ~70% of claims (based on ~31% allowed rate we saw in the data), and 69% of WSIAT appeals succeed, why aren't more workers appealing?

So we dug deeper. We downloaded WSIB's own data from their Safety Check tool:

- **Registered Claims (2020-2026)**: Total claims filed
- **Allowed Claims (2020-2026)**: Claims WSIB approved

The calculation is simple:

```
Denied = Registered - Allowed
Appeal Rate = WSIAT Appeals / Denied
Suppression Gap = Denied - WSIAT Appeals
```

**The results were stunning.**

## Who Are The 139,000 Workers Who Give Up?

They are:

### 1. **Workers who don't know they can appeal**
WSIB denial letters don't say: "69% of WSIAT appeals succeed—you should appeal."

They say: "Your claim has been denied. You have 6 months to object."

Most workers think "object" means complaining to WSIB again, not filing a formal WSIAT appeal.

### 2. **Workers who can't afford lawyers**
WSIAT is a quasi-judicial tribunal. The process is complex, adversarial, and intimidating. Most workers can't afford legal representation.

Community legal clinics exist but are overwhelmed. Wait times can be months. Many workers give up.

### 3. **Precarious workers**
- Gig workers
- Temporary foreign workers
- Part-time workers
- Workers with multiple jobs

They can't take time off to fight WSIB. They can't risk employer retaliation. They need money NOW, not 6-18 months from now after a WSIAT hearing.

### 4. **Immigrant workers**
- Language barriers
- Fear of government systems
- Cultural mistrust of formal institutions
- Don't know their rights

WSIB provides minimal translation services. WSIAT hearings are in English. Immigrant workers are disproportionately shut out.

### 5. **Workers who are exhausted**
By the time WSIB denies your claim, you've already:
- Been injured at work
- Lost income
- Dealt with medical appointments
- Filled out WSIB forms
- Waited months for a decision
- Been told "no"

The idea of starting over with a tribunal appeal? Many workers are simply too broken to fight.

## Cross-Tribunal Comparison: WSIAT vs HRTO

To contextualize WSIAT's 69% success rate, we also analyzed 62,093 decisions from the Human Rights Tribunal of Ontario (HRTO):

| Tribunal | Success Rate | Appeal Volume | Key Insight |
|----------|--------------|---------------|-------------|
| **WSIAT** | **69%** | 2,475/year | High success rate proves appeals work |
| **HRTO** | **2.66%** | Unknown | 88x worse outcomes than WSIAT |

**This proves WSIAT is actually effective.** The problem is not WSIAT—it's the barrier to reaching WSIAT.
